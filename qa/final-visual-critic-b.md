## Design criticism and debate with A

A and B agree that the hero split is legible and keeps the approved person intact. We also agree that improving route access is a stronger immediate usability step than changing the portrait or adding spectacle. The current split should not be described as delivering the cover composition promised in FINAL_PLAN.

The first chapter changes explanation and imagery for explicit traveler priorities, and only Apply writes the preference into the brief. That is meaningful interaction. However, a seat render beside three general bullets does not deliver the dominant itinerary analysis document proposed in the plan. A compact illustrative explanation of one concrete tradeoff would strengthen it, such as how a fixed arrival time changes the relevance of a connection. Counterargument: another checklist adds repetition and may imply actual flight results. Resolve by showing one conditional reasoning example, labelled illustrative, with no invented availability, prices or client evidence.

The generic wording compounds the template impression: “Your journey. Personally arranged”, “One person. Your whole journey”, “The fare is one part of the decision”, and “Look at the whole journey” repeatedly describe consideration without a specific example. Edit repeated introductions around the distinct task of each chapter. Counterargument: consistency improves comprehension. Keep common terminology for controls and service categories, while removing redundant promises.
