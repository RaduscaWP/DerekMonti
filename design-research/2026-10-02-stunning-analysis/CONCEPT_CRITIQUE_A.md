# Concept v1: independent critique A

Date: 2 October 2026. Inspected actual `concepts/concept-v1.png`. Analysis only; no application changes.

## Decision

**Reject v1 as the approval target.** Accept the clearly visible Business & First Class category, genuine portrait, route fields, consistent Fly with Derek masthead, and useful review subjects. The composition and some generated copy fail the conditions established in the debate.

### What fails

1. The giant promise plus headshot is the conventional consultant template identified in advance. A real face does not resolve generic composition by itself.
2. The original portrait's table and Derek's body visually continue into the cabin. Even without a literal face cutout, this reads as one photographic scene and suggests that he is onboard. A tiny Illustrative cabin view caption cannot reverse that main impression.
3. The first-scroll idea is useful, but equal icon columns, numbered factors, and an ornamental connector read like another reusable three-feature section. The work needs a more legible document structure, not additional decorative UI.
4. The generated ending “so you can arrive rested and ready” implies an outcome. “So there are no surprises later” is an unsupported assurance. Remove both. “Smoother from start to finish” also favors a result over a concrete explanation.
5. The mobile top gives substantial height to the portrait before category and proposition. It has a visible route starter, which is good; the next revision must not enlarge the image and displace that useful grouping.

## One precise revision

Make the opening an **editorial flight brief**, with the real person visibly responsible for it.

- Use a white left area, roughly 60% of the desktop width: readable Business & First Class label, dark navy heading, short service explanation, and compact From/To + Start planning entry. The actual brief is the focal foreground, rather than a large generic promise floating on photography.
- Use the original supplied portrait in a clearly bounded 3:4 right panel, retaining enough of the real gray background, clasped hands, and table to preserve its original setting. Give it a clear caption: Derek Monti · Your personal flight advisor. No blend, photographic continuation, cabin behind him, or image overlap.
- Move the illustrative cabin to a separate panoramic strip at the transition to the review. It belongs to the journey context, not the portrait's location. Keep its illustrative status readable. It should not become a second hero.
- Present the review as **one document/list**: the selected priority followed by the relevant consideration rows. Use readable labels such as Cabin on every segment, Arrival and connections, and Ticket conditions. Avoid three identical icon cards and a decorative branching line.
- On mobile, put category/proposition and the request entry together, with a compact bounded portrait beside or immediately after the short introduction. Do not create a portrait-only screen before the service and start action.

Use the headline **Your flight plans. Personally reviewed.** The Business & First Class label must remain sufficiently prominent to carry commercial premium-flight recognition. Introduce the preference options with **What matters most to you?** and use **Rest / Work / Travelling together** as priorities, not promised outcomes.

Suitable concrete review wording:

| Consideration | Explanation |
|---|---|
| Cabin on every segment | Derek considers the cabin across the full route, including connecting flights. |
| Arrival and connections | He reviews arrival time, connections, and practical changes between segments together. |
| Ticket conditions | He explains the stated change, cancellation, and fare conditions before you decide. |

These describe the review; they do not claim guaranteed comfort, arrival performance, adjacent seats, prices, or a successful booking. The use-priority action is valid only when it actually transfers that preference into the existing request flow.

## Acceptance of the revision

The new desktop and mobile pair must show the portrait's original setting separately from the cabin, retain the category and request entry, make the first scroll add concrete understanding, and contain no generated result promises. The white brief plus distinct portrait panel should be judged as a whole composition. Merely adding a border to v1's blended photo while leaving the same conventional hero is insufficient.

The image is an exploratory visual artifact. It does not establish working behavior, font fidelity, source approval, accessibility, or a verified responsive implementation.
