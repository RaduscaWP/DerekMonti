# Reviewer A: adversarial debate

Date: 2 October 2026. Scope: analysis only. No product edits. This follows the independent `CREATIVE_REVIEW_A.md` and a direct exchange with reviewer B.

## Decision with the current assets

**Lead with Derek → Your Journey.** Show the supplied portrait honestly in its original setting, identify commercial business/first class travel immediately, provide one obvious request path, and connect the person to a useful explanation of what is reviewed.

This is a conditional art-direction decision. No new direction has a desktop/mobile mockup or measured conversion result yet. It is not evidence that portraits convert better than cabin imagery. The decision is based on the personal service positioning, the current asset inventory, and the need to make a meaningful change from the existing cabin-led Home.

The current Home already has a persuasive passenger-window composition, the service category, a named advisor, a portrait, and a route starter. Source also contains a fare-audit ticket and a guidance folio. A new cabin hero and another itinerary scene would need substantial new composition and meaning to be different. With the supplied portrait already available, moving the named person into the visual lead is a more defensible new direction than pretending another window/folio combination is new.

## Strongest objection to B's cabin-first proposal

**It solves a category-recognition problem that the current Home already solves, while weakening the personal differentiation.** A beautiful cabin or arrival photograph is applicable to many premium agencies, airline sites, and travel publishers. Putting Derek and his process later can make this personal service more interchangeable.

B's good safeguards do not automatically make the concept new: one evidence-rich itinerary scene, restrained depth, tactile cards, and a line reveal are reasonable interaction choices. The current project already uses several of those narrative objects. A more polished combination could still be a rerun.

There is a serious asset issue too. Cabin-first puts maximum scrutiny on the cabin. The currently documented asset is illustrative. A new real commercial cabin scene would require sourcing, a coherent crop, and care not to imply a guaranteed airline/seat product. An airport apron or generic plane photograph is weaker: it establishes aviation, but says little about the traveler or the premium experience.

**What B gets right:** Cabin-first makes the travel category immediate and can communicate comfort more naturally than the supplied headshot. Its motion discipline is sound. A newly sourced, excellent commercial-cabin image with a clearly visible personal advisor and honest route entry could beat the person-first treatment if the latter remains conventional.

## Strongest objection to my person-first proposal

**A real face is not a distinctive concept by itself.** A cropped headshot on one side, a large generic promise on the other, a burgundy pill, and white cards beneath is a familiar consultant template. It could look like a financial advisor, coach, or private-jet broker until the traveler reads small supporting text.

The supplied portrait is warm and credible, but it does not show the work. Adding a fake desk, generated screens, an invented customer brief, a signature, or a cabin composite would create false documentary meaning. Using “Derek at Work” with this asset overstates what is shown. The selected name is Derek → Your Journey.

Three loosely related panels are another risk: portrait, cabin, and folio can become a collage. They need one compositional sequence and one useful relationship. For example, a visible traveler priority such as Arrive rested leads to an explanation that Derek considers cabin on each segment, the schedule, and connections. That demonstrates the comparison lens; it does not pretend to return a flight or quote.

The interactive review also risks distracting from the actual request. A decorative configurator must not collect answers that are lost when the traveler starts the real form. If it updates a preference, it should either share that preference with the existing request or be unambiguously labeled as an example before the interaction. Do not invent a backend implication.

**What the proposal gets right:** The personal service has a real named subject available now. It can differentiate the site without inventing industry credentials, guarantees, or a spectacular product object. The commercial category, request, and consequence still have to earn their place in the opening.

## Direct debate outcome

I challenged B directly on how cabin-first would differ from the existing Home. B replied that the nine captures do not establish a materially new cabin-first concept. B described its blind recommendation as category safety and motion discipline, rather than evidence of superiority, and conditionally moved toward Derek → Your Journey.

B's condition was that the first viewport visibly identify Business & First Class, retain one request action, and connect the person to decisions through composition rather than a chain of headings. B also identified the generic consultant risk and required a mobile composition that does not become a portrait screen followed by a copy screen.

I agree with those objections. I corrected B's description of the existing cabin as authentic: its manifest identifies an illustrative generated plate/Mixkit composite. We agree on a candidate for exploration after discussing those limits. **We have not independently validated the same finished visual concept, and we have no user/conversion evidence that resolves the lead choice.**

## What would change my mind

I would choose cabin-first if a paired desktop/mobile concept demonstrated all of the following better than the portrait-led candidate: immediate commercial premium-flight recognition, named personal responsibility, a clear request path, one readable decision consequence, and a genuinely different photographic composition from the current window.

A striking, newly sourced commercial-cabin scene could change that judgment. So could feedback from Derek's actual clients showing that the headshot-led opening feels like a general consultant rather than an aviation advisor. No such evidence is available in this round.

A real short film of Derek reviewing an owner-approved, anonymized travel brief would strengthen person-first substantially. That is a later asset opportunity. It is not permission to generate a false working photograph or claim a production capability that the current images do not show.

If the only person-first mock is a standard headshot/copy split, I would reopen **Flight Editorial** as the stronger alternative. Its route hierarchy and existing sourced destination photographs can create a coherent visual language without pretending the posed portrait documents work. It must still keep the named advisor and request visible so it does not become only a travel magazine.

## Binding acceptance conditions for the selected candidate

These are approval criteria for the proposed design, not a claim that they already pass.

1. **Paired evidence:** Provide a desktop opening and first scroll plus a deliberate mobile opening, using actual copy and the actual available portrait. A single hero render cannot establish the direction.
2. **Four visible answers:** A traveler should be able to identify who helps, that the service concerns commercial business/first class travel, how to request help, and one thing Derek reviews. Use a short comprehension check with actual people if possible. A five-second review is an evaluation target, not research already conducted.
3. **Honest photographic planes:** Retain Derek's supplied likeness and original setting. Keep cabin imagery separate. Label illustrative media appropriately; do not imply a specific airline product, confirmed booking, or documentary setting.
4. **One meaningful consequence:** A traveler priority may update a readable explanation of the comparison. It must not produce invented flight options, optimization scores, available fares, booking codes, schedules, or a confirmed recommendation.
5. **A clear request path:** Keep the modern existing request flow and its validation/security contracts. Any hero From/To start or preference entry connects clearly to that flow. Do not introduce a fake search affordance like the visible Blog strip.
6. **Mobile completeness:** Preserve the useful grouping demonstrated by capture 08. Category, person, request action, and enough context remain close together. Do not require a full-screen portrait before the traveler encounters the proposition and next step.
7. **A single identity:** Use one consistent masthead, navigation, terminology, and contact treatment across Home, Services, About, Journal, and the request. The present Home versus inner-page split is not part of the new direction.
8. **Useful cards:** Preserve flip and tilt, but make each card's information and next action meaningful. Touch and keyboard need explicit controls. Core explanations must remain available without hover. Tilting is optional, restrained, fine-pointer enhancement; card state changes remain usable with reduced motion.
9. **Motion budget:** No required pinned scroll or carousel reel. Limit the page to at most two major signature moments; a short supporting reveal is optional. B's conservative flip/tilt ranges may be starting points, but visual testing decides whether they feel appropriate. The still design must communicate the same information.
10. **No unsupported persuasion:** Do not base the concept on 15–60%, private fare access, 24/7, within-hours promises, affiliation, invented testimonials, or accreditation unless the factual register contains adequate approved evidence. Illustrative comparisons are not proof of achievable savings.
11. **Seven chapters only if useful:** The editing goal is distinct questions, not a magic count. Remove redundant introductions and repeated promises; preserve the actual request and supporting clarification.

With those conditions, Derek → Your Journey is the stronger lead to explore using the current assets. The opening and first-scroll mockups should be attacked again before implementation is approved.
