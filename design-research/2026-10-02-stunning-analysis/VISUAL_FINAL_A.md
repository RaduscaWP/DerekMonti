# Final independent visual review A

3 October 2026. Analysis only. Inspected `concepts/concept-v2.png` at original resolution and read `FINAL_PLAN.md`, `DEBATE_A.md`, `CONCEPT_CRITIQUE_A.md`, and the earlier creative critique. No application changes.

## Decision

**Approve v2 as a structural study; do not approve it as the finished visual direction or describe it as achieving the video's cinematic distinction.** The category, real person, request starter, and concrete review factors form a credible foundation. The composition remains a familiar consultant opening: large left headline, right portrait, form beneath copy, then a light feature section. A decorative line does not change that structure.

The next approval can validly authorize this content hierarchy and the person-led direction. A stronger static composition still needs a separate desktop/mobile checkpoint before full-page implementation. Assets and motion are work after confirmation, but they must not be treated as a guaranteed cure for a generic static layout.

## What v2 actually resolves

- Business & First Class is the largest message. Derek is named and the action is visible on desktop.
- The portrait has its own gray setting, hands and table. The cabin is separately placed and captioned. The misleading photographic continuation identified in v1 is gone in this study.
- Four readable considerations explain the review without fabricated prices, flight options, availability, scores, or outcome guarantees.
- The mobile study includes category, name, portrait and route entry close together. It is substantially more complete than a portrait-only mobile opening.

These are observations about the rendered study. They do not verify exact portrait preservation, licensed assets, fonts, responsive behavior, interaction or production performance.

## What still fails the intended art direction

**The subject is displayed rather than made into the composition.** The portrait says who Derek is; the adjacent text says what he offers. Their relation remains the relation in most professional-services templates. The first scroll adds content but does not yet create a distinctive aviation scene or editorial object. This is clearer design, not evidence of spectacle.

**PERSON → PRIORITY → REVIEW is internal framework language.** Travelers do not need to learn that model. The rail begins near the portrait and crosses empty space, but no state or piece of information visibly travels along it. Remove the labels and the ornamental rail. Any replacement line must identify an actual relationship, such as the selected Rest priority and its relevant consideration text. It must work without animation.

**The value is understated.** “Planned with Derek” plus comparison of cabins and connections can read as advisory planning only. It does not clearly explain whether he helps obtain a ticket/quote. Use one short, claim-register-approved sentence that states the real ticket service. Do not restore unsourced savings, access, timing or support promises to solve this problem. Keep the current explicit category headline unless testing a stronger composition requires a shorter version.

**The first-scroll hierarchy repeats the hero's marketing rhythm.** Another eyebrow and large promise consume the section before the useful review. “What matters most to you?” is the functional heading; promote it. The four factors should look like the continuation of a travel brief, not a white feature panel next to another stock window crop.

**Priority specificity remains unproven.** The displayed factors are generic and could appear unchanged for Rest, Work and Travelling together. Each option must change a concrete, relevant explanation. For example, Rest can emphasize timing and connection length; Work can explain cabin/work considerations; Together can discuss party requirements and seating requests subject to availability. Do not imply guaranteed sleep, productivity or adjacent seats. Preserve the explicit, optional transfer action.

## Exact next composition checkpoint

Develop **one editorial travel brief as the focal object**, with the real advisor clearly responsible for it. This must be visibly different from putting the same left/right blocks inside a new border.

1. Use a navy editorial cover with a full-width two-line `BUSINESS` / `& FIRST CLASS` masthead across the top. This spans the scene instead of preserving a left text column. Place one concise, named personal-review proposition in the lower-left margin and anchor one full-width route-entry slab along the bottom. Avoid fake boarding codes, stamps, signatures or fare-result decoration.
2. Position the original portrait as a bounded, unfeathered photographic plate offset center-right, below or into the masthead's negative space. Retain its gray setting, hands and table. Do not obscure the category letters, face/hands or request. The portrait is a deliberately separate plate; no invented working pose, cabin backdrop or implied onboard setting. Its placement must not recreate a balanced consultant split.
3. Continue the briefing language into a dominant first-scroll review document with priority selection and four consideration rows. Reduce the second giant heading. The selected priority should visibly connect to changed explanatory content; no ornamental branching diagram is needed.
4. Keep the cabin out of the hero. At first scroll it can occupy a narrow contextual strip, roughly a third of the scene at most, in relation to the dominant review document. It must not become another window hero. A better sourced photograph later is an asset improvement, not the concept itself.
5. Preserve the requested 3D service cards as a later tactile chapter. Do not make the hero a competing floating-card spectacle. The cards still need distinct fronts, useful backs and explicit touch/keyboard controls.

This is an art-direction proposal, not a demand for new technical scope. No need for a new renderer, backend capability, pinned scroll, or documentary media to demonstrate the static composition.

The posed portrait can support a striking editorial cover. To match the video's meaningful subject transformation more convincingly later, obtain a real directed portrait/short film of Derek reviewing an owner-approved, anonymized travel brief. The current photograph cannot document that work. Do not manufacture it with a generated desk, papers, hands or screens. The proposed cover earns a third composition study; it is not automatically accepted before that study is seen.

## Mobile and copy conditions

The current mobile portrait sits between the short proposition and the route fields, with the action appearing only after both fields. Its actual first-viewport position cannot be inferred from this montage. In the next 390px opening, retain one sentence explaining the ticket service and give the portrait a compact bounded placement beside or after the introduction; route start and its button must remain immediately discoverable. A tall photograph must not become the price of reaching the action.

The mobile study also drops the desktop clarification that fares and availability are confirmed separately. Preserve that meaning near the request entry in concise copy. Keep the category and person, readable route labels, one clear action, and sufficient service context together. Do not shrink them into microtype to force everything above an assumed fold.

## Acceptance boundary

**Direction/content approval: defensible now, with the above visual checkpoint recorded.**

**Final “stunning” composition approval: not earned by v2.** If no further composition study is produced before confirmation, present v2 honestly as a structure study and state that the final visual treatment remains to be developed. Do not promise that animation or better assets will automatically supply the video's distinction. Judge the next static desktop/mobile pair before building the remaining pages.

## V3 addendum: bounded approval of the proposed direction

Inspected `concepts/concept-v3.png` at original resolution. This supersedes v2 as the candidate mood study. No fourth generated board is needed for the analysis stage.

**Approvable:** the navy/white editorial mood, clear commercial Business & First Class category, separate portrait plate retaining its gray setting/table, named personal responsibility, and the prominent full-width white route-entry anchor. The meta PERSON → PRIORITY → REVIEW copy is removed. Four practical considerations remain readable; there are no invented fare results or outcome assurances. These are useful advances and give the owner concrete direction to approve.

**Still unresolved:** the generator has returned a left-copy/right-photo split rather than the proposed full-width masthead and offset portrait. Larger category type and darker color do not by themselves establish the subject-led distinction in the video. The display lettering also resembles a condensed face rather than the required Syne. The raster is not the final typographic or compositional specification.

Mandatory implementation corrections after confirmation:

1. **Composition:** use the agreed cover/spread approach: category type spanning the scene, original bounded portrait offset within deliberate negative space, short supporting proposition, route slab as the shared lower anchor. Avoid simply recreating this enlarged two-column poster. Keep category, portrait and action readable together. Review the actual static desktop opening before adding its motion or extending the treatment to other pages.
2. **Fonts and source assets:** implement the real Syne and DM Sans tokens, with readable line lengths and deliberate wrapping. Use the original portrait file separately from the layout. A generated rendering of that portrait does not establish pixel preservation or final photographic rights.
3. **Mobile order:** design an actual 390px opening. Put the short category/personal-service proposition and route starter/action ahead of a tall portrait; use a compact bounded portrait beside introductory copy or immediately after the entry. Keep Derek named above the action. Do not reproduce the montage's large image-before-form ordering without verifying the resulting viewport. The actual screenshot, not this board's apparent proportions, decides the first-viewport result.
4. **Request meaning:** preserve the statement that this starts a personal review and that fares/availability are confirmed separately on mobile as well as desktop. Use concise text near the route-entry action; the mobile board currently drops the second meaning. State the verified ticket-service proposition clearly rather than let “personally reviewed flights” imply an unexplained output.
5. **Illustrative review:** place “Illustration of the review process, not a live flight result” or equivalent directly adjacent to the priority controls on desktop and mobile. In v3 it sits below the desktop cabin image and is absent from the displayed mobile review. The illustration label must cover the whole interaction, not only the photograph.
6. **Meaningful states:** each priority changes a relevant explanation; no fabricated schedules, prices, seating outcome or recommendation. The separate “Use this priority in my request” action performs the optional transfer. A selection by itself remains exploratory. Remove the connecting line if it only points at a panel and conveys no useful state relationship.
7. **First-scroll editing:** reduce the second large promise where it delays “What matters most to you?” and the considerations. Keep one review spread, with cabin context subordinate. Actual readable text and interaction take precedence over matching the raster's tiny annotations.

**Final disposition:** v3 is a defensible visual direction study with explicit corrections. It is not a final validated design, proof of cinematic distinction, demonstrated responsive implementation, conversion evidence, or a claim of uniqueness. The owner can approve the content, mood and proposed cover approach now; the corrected real desktop/mobile composition is an implementation milestone. A real directed shoot remains a possible later improvement, not a prerequisite and not permission to fabricate documentary media.
