# Final independent visual review B

3 October 2026. Analysis only. No application source, product asset, provider setting, lead, deployment or build was changed. The only new file from this review is this report.

## Decision

**Retain Derek → Your Journey as the content direction; do not approve concept v2 as the final visual target.** V2 is clearer and more honest than the rejected first board, but its visible composition remains a polished conventional advisor template. It does not yet demonstrate the subject-led cinematic quality requested from the montage.

This is a judgment about observable composition, not a claim about AI authorship, market uniqueness or conversion. A static board also cannot establish motion quality, responsive behavior or the real request path.

## One final concrete candidate

**A navy editorial aviation cover, followed by one tactile review spread.** This changes the hierarchy and staging, rather than adding another effect to the same split hero.

For the desktop cover:

1. Use the existing navy, burgundy and white palette with Syne and DM Sans. Put a very large, fully readable `Business & First Class.` title across the upper width. The image must not obscure essential words.
2. Place the unchanged original portrait as the principal offset image plate beneath and into the title's negative space. Keep its gray background and table visibly bounded; no feathering into a cabin, no invented working pose, no chrome or particles. It should read as the central subject of a cover, not the right column of a two-column sales template.
3. Set the shorter `Planned with Derek.` proposition and supporting explanation in the lower-left negative space. Preserve the named identity and the existing factual boundaries.
4. Anchor a broad white From/To/Start planning slab across the lower edge as real interface foreground. The form is a usable control surface, not a simulated booking-result object. Keep labels, input contrast and the nearby service clarification readable.
5. Omit cabin imagery from the hero. It would add another focal plane and make this candidate harder to distinguish from the existing passenger-window composition.

The first scroll is one dominant review spread. The existing priority question and choices occupy one edge; a visibly bounded commercial-cabin strip supplies context; the four actual review factors form one considered document surface. Keep the cabin subordinate, and avoid another huge heading above four matching cards. The selected priority must visibly affect the explanatory emphasis. A thin decorative mark can connect the actual selection to those factors; abstract framework words are unnecessary.

On 390 CSS pixels, the intended order is masthead, category/title, one concise explanation, From/To/action, then the shorter portrait/context and readable stacked review. Do not shrink the desktop cover into miniature columns. The actual browser result must preserve the current mobile capture's person/category/request completeness.

Motion is one short settling of the cover's independent planes after its content is already visible, followed by a restrained priority explanation transition. No scroll pin, continuous drift, mandatory reveal or cinematic sequence before the request. Reduced motion uses the final static cover and instant explanation changes. Preserve the requested 3D service cards with explicit, readable controls.

This is a **candidate for a final composition board**, not a finding that the result already succeeds. Generate and inspect it once; do not promise spectacle in advance or continue cosmetic revisions indefinitely.

## The art-asset limit

The supplied portrait shows Derek posing at a table. It can honestly lead the brand, but it cannot document him reviewing a flight request. If the final cover still reads as an ordinary headshot poster, the most consequential improvement is a genuine directed photograph of Derek reviewing an anonymized, clearly illustrative travel brief: intentional side light, a readable working posture and negative space for the commercial category and request. That would require real photography and approved provenance. A generated fake documentary scene is not a substitute.

No new media is needed to evaluate the final cover candidate, and no real traveler data should appear in a future shoot or concept.

## Approval boundary

Before calling the proposed cover the implementation reference, separately inspect the actual desktop and narrow-screen artifacts for category/person/action clarity, image separation, useful review content and a materially different hierarchy. The rendered form must remain available immediately. The portrait must not be treated as evidence of work that the source photograph does not show.

Keep the implementation plan's factual, functional, 3D-card and accessibility constraints. Do not relax them to make a more dramatic board. If the new artifact fails the visual threshold, record that limit and the genuine-media recommendation instead of labeling a conventional composition stunning.

## V3 addendum: final bounded verdict

I inspected the actual `concepts/concept-v3.png` at original detail after the final editorial-cover study was produced. No fourth generation is recommended. No runtime behavior was tested for this addendum.

**V3 is a stronger composition study, but not an approved proof of the requested cinematic result.** The navy field, very large commercial-category title, larger portrait and broad white route-entry slab create stronger hierarchy and contrast than v2. The cabin is visibly separate in the first-scroll section. The abstract `PERSON → PRIORITY → REVIEW` label has been removed. Concrete review factors and an illustrative boundary appear on desktop, without invented prices, live inventory or guaranteed outcomes.

The remaining visual limitation is substantial: the generated result still uses a left headline/copy column beside a right rectangular posed headshot. It did not produce the proposed full-width masthead and offset central subject. Its condensed display type is also a different visual direction from the specified Syne system; the raster does not establish exact font fidelity. The first-scroll surface is clearer, but is still a familiar numbered list with a connector. I support the **navy editorial intent and content architecture conditionally**, not a claim that both critics approved this image as stunning.

The mobile study remains a raster column, not an actual 390 CSS pixel render. Its large portrait precedes the route entry again. The pictured first scroll ends after priority choices and the transfer link; it does not show the review factors or the illustrative note. Those elements may be intended below the crop, but their existence, order and readability cannot be inferred. The board therefore does not establish mobile completeness or mobile equivalence of the priority explanation.

### Mandatory implementation corrections

1. **Use the agreed design system and literal final copy.** Restore Syne and DM Sans through actual font assets and tokens. Reconcile the board's `Premium airline flights, personally reviewed.` with the plan's `Planned with Derek.` before coding; do not silently adopt generated wording. The visible category, named person, purpose and request action must remain unambiguous.
2. **Implement the editorial hierarchy deliberately.** Treat the board as a study of contrast and scale. Build the full-width category title and central offset portrait treatment where they remain readable. If the selected result retains a split layout, describe that honestly and review its quality; a condensed font alone is not a materially authored relationship.
3. **Make narrow-screen order real.** At actual 390 CSS pixels, place the concise category/person explanation and route action before the large photograph. Use a short identity treatment above the form if needed. Show a readable, priority-specific explanation and all relevant review factors in normal flow, with the illustrative boundary adjacent to that explanation. Do not reproduce the board by shrinking it.
4. **Use original source assets.** Insert the supplied portrait file directly in its bounded photographic setting. Do not crop identity information out, feather it into another room, reconstruct the hands/face or use the generated board as a documentary photograph. Keep the commercial-cabin context separate and accurately identified as illustrative unless a documented authentic asset replaces it.
5. **Preserve the explicit, private priority handoff.** Exploring Rested/work/together changes temporary explanatory state only. It does not submit a lead, persist a booking preference, send it to a provider or emit preference analytics. Only `Use this priority in my request` deliberately transfers the choice to the existing request flow. The board does not prove this behavior; implementation must enforce it.
6. **Keep the illustrative review distinct from flight output.** No fabricated itinerary, price, availability, rating or guaranteed arrival outcome may appear to supply visual drama. The existing real form must preserve its validation and actual receipt/error semantics. A request-start action is not a confirmed booking.
7. **Give motion and 3D their defined jobs.** A short optional entrance cannot hide or delay copy/form controls. Priority changes preserve focus and remain instant with reduced motion. Keep the requested service-card flip/tilt with visible touch/keyboard controls; neither this static board nor a planned transition proves that those interactions work.

The bounded delivery is now sufficient to discuss and confirm the content direction, navy editorial intent and execution plan. It is insufficient to claim the exact final cinematic quality has already been achieved. Verify that quality on the real implementation before treating it as complete. If the photographic result still reads as a conventional headshot poster, the genuine directed shoot described above remains the meaningful asset improvement; further generated cosmetic boards are not a substitute.
