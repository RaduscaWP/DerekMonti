# Concept v1: independent critique B

Date: 2 October 2026. Analysis only. Inspected the actual `concepts/concept-v1.png` at original detail with the local image viewer. No app or asset was edited. This report assesses the image concept, not a running interface.

## Verdict: reject as the approval concept

The board improves personal presence and offers a legible route entry, but it does not yet deliver the distinctive person → priority → review relationship. The desktop uses a familiar large headshot beside marketing text. Derek, the table, the cabin seat and the window read as one photographic scene. A tiny “Illustrative cabin view” label does not adequately separate that perceived scene. This conflicts with the requirement to retain the real posed portrait honestly in its own setting.

The actual board also diverges from the selected copy. Its headline is “Premium flights. Handled personally.” The approved candidate is “Business & First Class. Planned with Derek.” The current headline and conventional portrait split could belong to many travel advisers or concierge businesses.

The first-scroll section introduces the right idea, but three identical icon/text cells and a branching line are a common feature-grid treatment. The detail does not feel sufficiently connected to the opening composition. “Your priorities shape the review” is a statement; the traveler question “What matters most to you?” is a clearer interaction prompt.

## Specific failures and retained strengths

| Element | Finding | Decision |
|---|---|---|
| Genuine person | Derek is a clear dominant subject, with a pleasant professional expression. | Keep the original portrait and its original gray setting. Do not alter identity, pose, clothing, hands or environment. |
| Hero composition | The large face and adjacent text reproduce a consultant split layout; cabin fades into his setting. | Recompose, do not merely enlarge the face or window. |
| Category and copy | Business/First is an eyebrow while the large copy is generic. The selected exact H1 was replaced. | Restore exact category in the dominant headline. |
| Request | From/To and Start planning form a clear entry. | Keep. Avoid an additional hero button with the same job. |
| Mobile | Category, name, From/To and action are present, but the top portrait consumes considerable height and the cabin context disappears. | Put category/action first; use a short, clearly separated two-image context strip in the remaining view. |
| Unsupported outcomes | “so you can arrive rested and ready,” “make the journey smoother from start to finish,” and “so there are no surprises later” turn a review framework into implied outcomes. | Replace with neutral descriptions of what is checked. |
| Controls | EN selector was invented for an English-only site. First-scroll cards do not show an implemented selection mechanism in this static board. | Remove the language selector. Mock native, explicit selection states. |
| Image provenance | The cabin is an illustration. The real supplied portrait's owner identity/rights remain governed by the source log. | Keep separate captioned planes and existing provenance boundaries. |

## One precise compositional revision

Use **two visibly separate photographic plates joined by one continuous review rail**. This is one structural revision, not a new effects package.

On desktop, keep the unchanged posed portrait as a substantial rectangular plate with its real gray background. Place it next to a clear navy field for the exact headline and route entry. Place the illustrative commercial-cabin image in a smaller, deliberately inset landscape rectangle toward the journey side, separated by a visible navy or white gutter and its own caption. Neither photograph fades into the other. No shared table edge, shadow, seat, window, arm or reflection connects them as a fake scene.

Below the opening, one slender burgundy line continues through the layout in normal document flow. It starts beside the visible caption **Derek Monti · Personal flight advisor**, leads to **What matters most to you?** and the existing priority choice, then continues into the readable review factors at the first scroll. The line's job is a reading connection. It is decorative, not a flight route, live result or technical workflow diagram. Do not build a pinned scroll reel around it.

The relation is now visible across both frames: a person receives a preference and applies a comparison lens. The cabin is the context of that preference, not the room where the portrait claims he was photographed. A larger portrait, different crop, extra gradient or more icons alone would not satisfy this revision.

On 390px, begin with the same masthead, then the exact category headline, one short supporting sentence and the From/To request entry. The action should be visible by approximately 550 CSS pixels from the top, subject to actual text metrics and header height. Follow with a short portrait/context strip containing two separated, captioned image plates; the portrait is the larger plate. The rail then descends into the priority choice. This avoids an entire portrait-only first screen and preserves the existing mobile capture's category/action completeness. Do not pretend the static image proves that pixel budget; confirm it in the eventual browser implementation.

## Copy to put in the revised board

Identity: **DEREK MONTI · PERSONAL FLIGHT ADVISOR**

H1 line 1: **Business & First Class.**  
H1 line 2: **Planned with Derek.**

Supporting: **Share your route, dates and what matters to you. I’ll help you compare the cabin, connections and ticket conditions.**

Labels/action: **From** · **To** · **Start planning**

Priority heading: **What matters most to you?**

Choices: **Arrive rested** · **Ready to work** · **Travel together**. These presentation labels retain existing `rested`, `work`, and `together` stored values.

Selected explanation: **Rest on a long flight and a considered arrival time matter most.**

Review lead: **For that priority, Derek reviews:**

- **Cabin on every segment**: The cabin and seat type across the whole itinerary.
- **Departure and arrival timing**: How the proposed timing fits the traveler's plans.
- **Connections**: Connection length, airport changes and the complexity of the route.
- **Ticket conditions**: The conditions attached to each option, including changes and flexibility.

Boundary: **Illustrative review. Fares and availability are confirmed separately.**

Use literal copy in the approval board. Any generator's added wording must be checked and removed before presenting the board for approval. The factors are explanations, not values calculated from a real booking search. Selection changes only the explanation and the existing trip preference.

## Recheck before calling the revision approved

- At desktop size, the images cannot be mistaken for one documentary photograph.
- The opening prominently names Derek and commercial Business & First Class travel, with a visible request entry.
- At 390px, category and action precede a long image; copy is readable without miniature review columns.
- First scroll visibly connects a chosen priority to specific review factors, rather than an interchangeable benefits grid.
- One shared masthead, no invented language selector, no unsupported results, discounts, schedules, guarantees or credentials.
- The design still works with a still image and an instant priority change. Nothing requires canvas, a scroll pin or an animation to be understood.

This is a rejection of the current visual proposal, not a claim that the generator or any designer lacks authorship. A revised image can establish composition and copy only; keyboard, touch, reduced motion, hydration, quote behavior and performance remain separate acceptance work after implementation is authorized.
